Case Study: 3D Horde Shooter (Godot)

The Problem: I needed 60+ enemies on screen at a time with their own collisions and pathing without tanking Godot's performance.

The Solution: I built a tiny, lightweight boid-style system, where the game world is divided into smaller cells. Each enemy checks which cell it's in and if there are any other enemies inside that cell. If there are, the enemies slightly 'push' them away without any actual collisions calculating, ensuring great performance with fully 3D modeled and animated characters.
